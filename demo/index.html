Six schedules that look fine and break silently.

  1. Berlin, every night at 02:30 FAIL
    The most ordinary cron in Europe. On the last Sunday of March the hour between 02:00 and 03:00 does not exist, so the nightly job is skipped -- and because it is daily, the next run is 47 hours later.
  2. Santiago, every night at 00:00 FAIL
    Chile moves its clocks at midnight, so the midnight run itself disappears on the spring-forward Sunday. A 'daily' backup silently becomes non-daily once a year.
  3. Lord Howe Island, 02:15 daily FAIL
    A 30-minute DST shift: 02:00 jumps to 02:30. A job at 02:15 falls inside the half-hour hole and vanishes, which 60-minute reasoning never predicts.
  4. Samoa, 2011-12-30 (a day that never happened) FAIL
    Samoa jumped across the date line: the whole of 2011-12-30 was skipped. Every schedule in Pacific/Apia lost that day, and a yearly December 30 job lost a year.
  5. February 29, every year FAIL
    A yearly recurrence anchored on a leap day can wait eight years: 2096 -> 2104, because 2100 is not a leap year. 'Yearly' freshness is a promise the calendar cannot keep.
  6. Berlin, 02:30, 'fire on both' policy FAIL
    The autumn clock change makes 02:00-03:00 happen twice. A scheduler configured to fire on both occurrences runs the job twice in one night -- which a once-per-day contract catches and a dashboard does not.
  7. cron '0 0 1 * 1' -- the day-field trap PASS
    Cron ORs day-of-month and day-of-week when neither field is '*'. The intent was 'the 1st, if it is a Monday'; the effect is 'the 1st, and every Monday'.
